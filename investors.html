The opportunity

Bodies are ready; minds are not

Robot and drone hardware gets better and cheaper every year. What most makers lack is the mind that makes a robot useful.

Thousands of makers are left out

The best-funded robot AI companies train their own large models at great cost and work with the largest partners. The many small and medium makers have no AI team.

Customers want to teach, not program

Setup and programming, not hardware, hold many buyers back. They want to tell a robot what to do, and correct it when it gets something wrong.

What we're building

Kith keeps a general vision-language model as the robot's thinking core, but never puts it in the real-time loop. For each job, Kith builds small, fast parts, such as a quick vision model or a practice-based skill tuner, and checks every step with the robot's own cameras and sensors.

People teach it with words, manuals, drawings and advice. It asks when it is unsure, and keeps getting better with practice. What it learns stays readable, so people can check and correct it.

How Kith works

Why Kith can win

Taught, not trained

No fleets of demonstration data and no retraining for each new task. Robots learn by being taught, which keeps Kith capital-light.

Rides the model curve

Kith works with any leading vision-language model, in the cloud or on site. Every better model makes Kith better, without retraining anything.

A market the giants don't serve

We start with small and medium makers, then work up. A product simple enough for a maker with no AI team is easy for a large maker too.

Neutral by design

We make no robots, so makers can trust us with their products. We win when they win.

Learning that compounds

What Kith learns about a robot's body and a job is kept, so the next skill, and the next robot, starts ahead.

The business model: the PC playbook

Microsoft didn't build PCs. It made it possible for Dell and HP to build them, and earned on every one.

Kith follows the same playbook: a license on each robot that ships with Kith, plus services such as setup and ready-made teaching packs. Our first design partners get free licenses, so Kith gets onto many robots quickly. Terms are being set together with those first partners.

Where we are

Kith is a young product, moving quickly from simulation to real robots.

A physical skill, learned from a coach's words Simulated in MuJoCo

Two robot arms learned to juggle three balls from a one-sentence task, practice, and plain advice from a coach who could not juggle. In the end, 19 of 20 new tries reached 12 catches in a row, with no AI model retrained.

Learning that carries over Simulated in MuJoCo

What one arm learned helped a second arm learn a new, gentle catch in 76 throws. Without that knowledge, 612 throws were not enough.

Reading instructions made for people Simulated in MuJoCo

Kith read a four-panel drawing of a knot, turned it into exact steps, and asked an expert where the drawing was unclear.

Next: real robots, with design partners

Mapping and patrol, many robots and drones under one mind, and a kit for connecting makers' robots are in development. We are now taking Kith onto makers' real robots.

Why now

The models are ready

Vision-language models can now recognize things they were never trained on, read drawings, and reason about plans. With the right harness for speed and precision, that is enough to be a robot's mind.

Hardware is everywhere

Capable, affordable robot arms, mobile robots and drones come from many makers. They need minds to stand apart.

Physical AI is the next platform

The race to build robot brains has begun. The open question is who gives the thousands of makers outside the giants a mind of their own.
